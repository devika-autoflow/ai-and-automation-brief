## 1. Top 3 AI Products Trending Today

### 🟦 Gemini App — Google's assistant just crossed 1 billion users
\n
What it is\n
Gemini is Google's version of ChatGPT — an AI you talk or type to on your phone or computer that answers questions, writes things, and now does tasks for you.
\n
What it actually does\n
You ask it anything — write an email, plan a trip, explain your kid's math homework, edit a photo — and it does it right there in the app. A lot of people (63%) actually talk to it out loud instead of typing, like a voice assistant that's actually smart.
\n
Why people are excited\n
It just became Google's **fastest-growing product ever** — the 14th Google product to ever hit 1 billion users, and it got there faster than Search, Maps, or Gmail did. It's also now generating over 150 million photos a day for people. That's a signal Google has closed the gap with ChatGPT (which hit 1 billion earlier this summer).
\n
Who this matters to\n
Anyone with an Android phone or Google account already has this — no extra app needed. For businesses, it means the "AI assistant" is no longer a niche tool; it's now as normal as a search bar, so customers increasingly expect AI-level convenience everywhere.
\n
Source: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/11/googles-gemini-app-surges-to-one-billion-users/)

### 🟩 Lovable — "describe your app, we'll build it" just raised $400M
\n
What it is\n
Lovable is a website where you type a plain-English description of an app or website you want, and AI builds the actual working software for you — no coding required. This is often called **"vibe coding"** (meaning: you describe the vibe/idea and the AI writes the real code behind it).
\n
What it actually does\n
Type something like "build me a booking site for my dog-walking business with payments" and it generates a real, working website/app in minutes that you can keep editing just by describing changes.
\n
Why people are excited (and some are nervous)\n
Investors just valued the company at **$13.3 billion** — double what it was worth in December — after it raised $400M. Its revenue has nearly tripled and is projected to hit $600M a year by the end of this month. Excitement: non-coders can now build real software. The nervous part: professional developers are watching a tool built by a company with under 500 employees compete with software agencies that used to charge six figures for the same work.
\n
Who this matters to\n
Small business owners, solo founders, and non-technical people who need a website, internal tool, or app but can't afford (or don't want to hire) a developer.
\n
Source: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/12/lovable-confirms-new-13-3b-valuation-raises-another-400m/)

## 3. One Pain Point I Can Solve
\n
### 😤 "I'm drowning in repetitive admin work and don't have time to fix it"
\n
The problem, in plain words\n
Across real estate, staffing, e-commerce, and local services, the same complaint keeps showing up: business owners and their staff spend hours every day on things a computer should be doing — typing the same info into two systems, chasing documents, answering the same five customer questions, generating invoices one at a time. One example from this week's research: a business processing 40–50 orders a day was losing **3–4 hours daily** just generating invoices by hand.
\n
Why this pain exists (root cause)\n
Most small businesses run on a patchwork of separate tools — email, spreadsheets, a CRM, maybe a booking app — that don't talk to each other. Someone has to be the "human glue" copying information between them. That's not a skills problem, it's a **missing connection** problem — and that's exactly what automation tools like n8n are built to fix.
\n
How to solve it with n8n + Claude (step by step)\n
\n1. **Map the repetitive task** — sit with the owner for 30 minutes and write down every step they do by hand (e.g., "new order comes in → check inventory → generate invoice → email customer → update spreadsheet").
\n2. **Build the trigger** in n8n — connect to wherever the task starts (inbox, form, spreadsheet, CRM) so the workflow kicks off automatically the moment new work arrives.
\n3. **Add Claude as the "brain"** — plug in a Claude step to read the incoming email/document, understand what's being asked, and draft the right response, categorize it, or pull out the key details (dates, amounts, names).
\n4. **Connect the output** — have n8n push the result into the tools they already use (send the invoice, update the CRM, post the Slack message) so nothing changes about how the team works day-to-day.
\n5. **Add a human-approval step** for anything customer-facing or money-related at first, so the owner trusts it before going fully hands-off.
\n6. **Test on real examples for a week**, then remove the approval step for the parts that are working perfectly.\n
\n
Who to sell this to, and what to charge\n
Best-fit customers: real estate agencies, staffing/recruiting firms, e-commerce sellers doing 20+ orders a day, and local service businesses (clinics, salons, contractors) with a shared inbox that's always behind. These are businesses with real repetitive volume but no in-house developer.
\n
Pricing benchmark: the staffing-firm example above spent about **$3,000** on the initial build and is saving roughly **$72,000/year** — a strong, easy-to-pitch ROI story. A reasonable structure: **$1,500–$5,000** one-time build fee depending on complexity, plus **$150–$400/month** for maintenance and monitoring (since workflows need occasional fixes as the business's tools change).
