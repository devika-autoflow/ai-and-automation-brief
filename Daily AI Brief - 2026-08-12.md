## 3. One Pain Point I Can Solve
\n
\n
The problem, in plain wordsPeople genuinely hate AI customer service chatbots. Nearly 1 in 5 people who've dealt with an AI support bot say it gave them zero benefit — it gave confident-sounding but useless answers, sent irrelevant help-article links, and made it hard to ever reach an actual human. This isn't just annoying customers — it's costing the businesses money: **1 in 4 business owners say AI is actively costing them clients.**
\n
Real complaint
\n
"I hate customer-service chatbots" — a common consumer refrain, per CNBC's reporting on the rocky AI-refund experience.
\n
Why this happens (the root cause)Most businesses bought or built a generic chatbot that was trained only on FAQ pages, with no logic for "this person is frustrated, hand them to a human now" and no memory of the customer's order history or past conversations. So it loops the customer through the same unhelpful answers instead of escalating. Research shows about 38.8% of the time, a fully AI-handled interaction fails outright — the fix isn't "remove AI," it's "use AI to make the human handoff smarter and faster."
\n
How to solve it — step by step with n8n + Claude
\n
\n1. Every incoming support message (email, chat, or form) triggers an n8n workflow.
\n2. Claude reads the message and classifies it: simple question, needs account/order lookup, or "customer is frustrated / issue is complex."
\n3. For simple questions, Claude answers directly, but only using the business's real docs and policies (not guessing) — and always with a clear, one-click way to say "still stuck? talk to a person."
\n4. For anything complex or where the customer sounds upset, n8n instantly pulls their order/account history and past conversation from the CRM, and posts it all — question plus context — into a Slack or email alert for a real staff member, so the human isn't starting from zero.
\n5. Every conversation gets logged automatically to a simple dashboard so the business owner can see what's being asked and where the bot is failing, and improve it over time.\n
\n
Who to sell this to, and what to chargeSmall e-commerce stores, local service businesses, and small SaaS companies with fewer than 10 support staff — they're big enough to be drowning in repetitive tickets but too small to have proper support tooling. Charge roughly **$1,500–$3,000 to build and set it up**, plus **$200–$500/month** to maintain and tune it as their FAQs/policies change. That maintenance fee is the real business — it turns a one-off project into recurring revenue.
