## 3. One Pain Point I Can Solve
\n
\n
### "My tools don't talk to each other, so I retype everything three times"
\n
The problem, in plain words
\n
Small business owners run lean — often one person handles sales, billing, and scheduling in the same afternoon. Their CRM, accounting software, and calendar were each bought separately to solve one specific problem, so they don't connect. Every new customer or order means someone manually copying the same details into two or three different systems. Business owners describe it as: "systems weren't broken individually — they were purchased separately," and now nobody in the company is the glue holding the data together. It quietly eats **5–10 hours a week** of a skilled person's time doing what a computer should be doing.
\n
Why this pain exists (root cause)
\n
Each app was built by a different company to do one job well (billing, or scheduling, or customer records) — none of them were designed to automatically share data with each other out of the box. Without someone actively connecting them, a human has to be the bridge.
\n
How to solve it with n8n + Claude (step by step)
\n\n
- Ask the business owner to list every place the same piece of information gets typed twice (e.g., a new customer's name goes into the CRM, then the invoicing tool, then a spreadsheet).\n
- Build one n8n workflow that "watches" the first tool for a trigger — like a new lead form submission or a new signed deal.\n
- Add a Claude step inside that workflow to clean and standardize the incoming data (fix inconsistent name formats, split full addresses into fields, categorize the deal type) since raw form data is usually messy.\n
- Have n8n automatically push that cleaned data into the other 2–3 tools using their built-in connections (most CRMs, accounting tools, and calendars already support this).\n
- Add a simple Slack or email notification step confirming it happened, so the owner never has to double-check manually.\n
\n
Who to sell this to, and what to charge
\n
Best targets: small businesses with 10–30 employees running 3+ disconnected tools — real estate agencies, medical/dental clinics, contractors, and e-commerce shops. Charge a **$500–$1,500 one-time setup fee** per workflow, plus **$100–$300/month** for monitoring and fixes when an app updates its system. A strong pitch: "I'll save you 5–10 hours a week of retyping, guaranteed, or you don't pay."
